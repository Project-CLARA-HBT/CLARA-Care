# Can local covariance adaptation improve conditional interpolation?

The daily bridge removed average diurnal shape, but local shrinkage tied a training-only profile. A third development comparison tests whether changes in the observed *shape of temperature fluctuations* favor different fits at different outages. Use the same two equipment records, training window, mask design, primary macro MAE and paired four-week resampling. Previous test weeks are development data for this pass. Keep every previous comparator.

For each station and length, reconstruct the missing vector from the 24 adjacent hourly temperatures on either side and the training-only clock mean. Learn ridge regression on training gaps to predict deviations from the training-profile bridge. Features are deviations of both flanks from a straight line extended through the two observed anchors with training daily shape removed, the two anchor levels, the gap's overall slope, and sine/cosine of the start clock. Standardize features on training only. Compare training-only daily bridge, a pooled ridge and k-nearest-neighbor average of training residual vectors (k=16, standardized Euclidean distance). Candidate: fit two separate ridge experts to training cases split at the median observed flank volatility and apply the matching expert. Define volatility as the RMS first difference of each observed flank after subtracting training clock shape. Compare also with a soft mixture of the two expert predictions using continuous distance to training median scaled by training interquartile range (explicit ablation); select hard routing as candidate before scoring. All ridge penalties use 30 on the summed standardized least-squares objective. There is no tuning or hyperparameter selection. Give every arm the same temperature measurements; retain the more informed forestry arms with load channels in the previous study. Mixtures of experts, kernel smoothing and conditional interpolation are established. A regional improvement alone cannot establish novelty.
