# Locally estimated daily shape between observed temperature anchors

Development amendment after the initial forest comparison. The linear estimator was strongest on the first station and short outages. The seasonal estimator adds information for some longer outages on the second station. Fit a daily temperature profile from the three days before and three days after each gap, using only observed hours. Subtract each day's mean before estimating the 24-hour profile. Subtract the linear interpolation of the profile at the two anchors from its predicted profile inside the gap. Adding this correction to the observed-endpoint line preserves both endpoint values and recovers a stable additive daily cycle superimposed on a local linear drift. Neither linear nor unadjusted seasonal interpolation exactly reconstructs that family in general. Periodic adjustment and interpolation are established techniques. Perfect reconstruction of this restricted model is a verification case, not a novelty claim.

Compare the fixed six-day local profile, a narrower two-day profile, a global training-only daily profile, linear interpolation, the registered shifted seasonal average, and both original forests. The candidate attenuates the six-day correction by its cross-day reliability: split six days into two alternating sets, center each set's clock profile, and divide their mean product by their mean squared energy averaged over sets. Clip the factor to [0,1] and use zero for zero energy. No tuning. Score the exact original masks, keep the original primary outcome and 5% margin with paired 4-week bootstrap intervals. Use the development data only. Require a win over every comparator including the simpler six-day profile before further independent evaluation. A short source series, less than three days of flanking history, real missing covariates and station-disjoint deployment are outside this pass.
