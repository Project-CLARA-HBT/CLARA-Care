{\Large\bfseries From Longitudinal Context to Safe Action: A Co-Versioned Governance Contract with Layer~1 Deterministic Safety Barrier for Primary Care AI}\\[0.7em]
\textbf{Nguyen Ngoc Thien, Trinh Minh Quang}\\
Hai Ba Trung High School, Hue, Vietnam\\
Corresponding author: \texttt{kakangocthien109@gmail.com}
\end{center}
\textbf{Topic area:} AI and Digital Transformation in Healthcare and Medical Education

\textbf{Background.} In primary care and family medicine, longitudinal electronic health records encompass multi-encounter clinical histories, chronic disease management trajectories, and sensitive personal health data. A persistent primary care AI system may read an authorized patient snapshot, but return a proposed clinical action or writeback only after the record, patient consent, or clinical policy has changed (time-of-check to time-of-use / TOCTOU drift). Furthermore, feeding complete unminimized health records to large language models (LLMs) risks severe Protected Health Information (PHI) over-disclosure and exposes clinicians and patients to LLM hallucinations during temporal arithmetic (such as preventive screening intervals and immunization due dates) and drug-drug interaction (DDI) evaluations.

\textbf{Methods.} We report the GLHS (Governed Longitudinal Health Snapshot) architecture implemented in CLARA-Care for family medicine and primary care workflows. GLHS introduces a \textbf{Dual-Layer State Barrier}: (1) a \textbf{Layer~1 Deterministic Reconciliation Kernel} (a trusted, non-LLM engine at the API gateway tier in Python/PostgreSQL) that deterministically evaluates bitemporal validity ($\tau_{\text{valid}} \cap \tau_{\text{known}}$), calculates protocol due-window arithmetic (preventive screening recall schedules, chronic care due dates, and immunization grace periods) using deterministic temporal math without LLM hallucinations, checks severe drug-drug interactions, verifies dynamic patient consent and role permissions, and compiles a cryptographic Merkle digest ($\Pi_{\text{Merkle}}$) into a Task-Bounded Health State Snapshot (THSS) enforcing task-scoped data minimization; and (2) a \textbf{Layer~2 Epistemic Arbiter} (LLM) isolated from raw temporal math to focus strictly on qualitative synthesis. Model proposals retain the exact THSS binding to a Governed State Transition (GST) that re-verifies state and governance freshness before writeback. We evaluated GLHS using deterministic contract tests, PostgreSQL concurrency matrices ($N=12$ schedules), a frozen 64-subject synthetic cohort across Claude Sonnet 4.6 and Gemini 3.6 Flash High ($1{,}152$ repeated model-condition evaluations), and an independent sealed 384-subject primary care cohort under a paired subject-level diagnostic framework.

\textbf{Results.} In the subject-level paired evaluation ($N=384$ subjects, 3,456 solver cells), Strict THSS yielded a null decision delta against full authorized history: 70 wins, 73 losses, 241 ties ($\hat{\Delta} = -0.781\%$, $SE=3.12\%$, 95\% bootstrap CI $[-6.77\%, +5.21\%]$, exact sign test $p=0.867$). Because this empirical confidence interval crosses the prespecified $\pm 2.0\%$ margin, the study is underpowered at $N=384$ to confirm statistical non-inferiority or equivalence (which would require $N \ge 7{,}500$). Rather than claiming zero clinical decision degradation or Pareto dominance, context minimization delivered an 87.4\% prompt token reduction (mean 412 vs.\ 3,280 tokens, $p < 10^{-40}$) and a 68.2\% latency reduction while the clinical decision accuracy delta remained within empirical variance. Simultaneously, the framework enforced task-scoped data minimization (0.0\% unintended sensitive entity exposure in the evaluated suite) and eliminated read-to-write TOCTOU state corruptions across the evaluated test suite ($0/256$ invalid commits admitted vs.\ $256/256$ by unbound baseline, exact McNemar $p = 1.727 \times 10^{-77}$). The Layer~1 non-LLM safety kernel executed temporal due-window calculations deterministically. In the 64-subject cohort, Strict THSS achieved all-axis exact match for 63/64 Claude subjects (98.44\%) and 64/64 Gemini subjects (100\%). In the 12-schedule PostgreSQL TOCTOU matrix, zero forbidden commits occurred across consent revocations, role transitions, policy updates, and concurrent state drifts. Bounded finite-state exploration verified 21,361 states and 90,432 transitions to depth 5 with zero invariant violations.

\textbf{Conclusion.} GLHS demonstrates that a Layer~1 non-LLM safety barrier and co-versioned disclosure-to-commit contract prevent read-to-write state inconsistencies and enforce task-scoped data minimization in primary care workflows while maintaining clinical decision quality within empirical variance. Current evidence is derived from synthetic cohorts and software benchmarks; clinical deployment requires prospective real-world clinical validation.

\textbf{Keywords:} primary care informatics; family medicine AI; data minimization; task-bounded context; non-LLM safety barrier; state consistency; clinical governance; concurrency safety.
