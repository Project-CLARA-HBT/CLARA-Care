## 1. Paper title and target reader

**Title (from the blueprint, unchanged):** *Dependency-Complete Optimistic Admission for Concurrent Clinical AI Writes*.

**Target reader:** distributed-systems and database-concurrency researchers and practitioners building multi-tenant clinical AI platforms — readers of COMPSAC's systems track who implement transactional write admission, optimistic concurrency control (OCC), or authorization-gated persistence, and who must decide between (a) coarse profile-wide versioning, (b) DB-native SERIALIZABLE, and (c) application-level dependency scoping. Secondary readers: safety engineers who must certify that AI-generated clinical writes cannot commit on stale dependencies under changing consent and policy epochs.

## 2. Scope paragraph (TRULY DISTINCT endpoint)

The truly distinct endpoint of this study is **automatically derived, semantically required cross-resource dependencies for AI-generated clinical writes, quantified as a safety/throughput frontier**. Silo-style OCC and PostgreSQL Serializable Snapshot Isolation already solve concurrency control in general; neither derives, per write operation, which cross-resource reads (medication, allergy, history, purpose grants, consent and policy epochs) are semantically required for that operation's admissibility. The proposed mechanism freezes a per-operation dependency schema and required read/write edges, validates every required edge version and consent/policy epoch in a single database transaction under canonical lock ordering, and exports the resulting histories to an independent checker. The measured frontier is two-sided: fewer unnecessary aborts (oracle-defined false-stale rejections) **without** more forbidden commits, and the fanout/contention point at which dependency-scope overhead erases throughput gains relative to fully serializable PostgreSQL. No claim is made of a new isolation algorithm, a new authorization system, or a new anomaly checker; the claim is a derivation method plus its quantified frontier for AI-generated clinical writes.

## 4. Research questions (exactly three, blueprint substance)

The three RQs below are the blueprint's RQ1–RQ3, restated with preregistered endpoints and falsifiers. Primary scientific unit: one independent logical schedule (independent history); never per-event pseudo-replication. Development/holdout split and denominators are frozen in `COMPSAC-rq_registry`.

### RQ1 — Dependency-complete read set vs globally versioned OCC

**RQ1 (blueprint):** Compared with globally versioned OCC, does a dependency-complete read set reduce unnecessary aborts without increasing forbidden commits?

- **Primary endpoint:** oracle-defined false-stale rejection rate under matched independent schedules (safety co-endpoint: forbidden commit rate, which must remain zero for the candidate arm under complete independently labeled schedules).
- **Strongest competing baseline:** B1 Silo-style entity readset OCC with commit-time validation (it also scopes validation to read sets and could plausibly deliver the same abort reduction without semantic dependency derivation); B0 global-profile version OCC is the directly named comparator in the question.
- **Independent oracle:** dependency-closure oracle plus transaction-history consistency checker implemented separately from the candidate (no candidate import; truth labels fixed before arms run, per `CASE_MATRIX.md`), with Elle (`elle20`) as the black-box history checker on exported histories.
- **Statistical test:** paired sign-flip permutation test on per-schedule false-stale rates across matched schedule pairs (cluster = independent schedule), ≥5 independent seeds per setting before power decisions; paired binary commit outcomes compared with McNemar's exact test; bootstrap confidence intervals reported for all rate contrasts.
- **Falsification condition:** any forbidden commit by the candidate arm under a complete, independently labeled dependency schedule falsifies the safety claim; absence of a false-stale reduction relative to B0/B1 is a null result reported candidly, and the RQ1 benefit claim is withdrawn rather than re-framed.

### RQ2 — Omitted-dependency failure propagation and separate-oracle detection

**RQ2 (blueprint):** How does omitted dependency failure propagate under explicit write-skew and policy-epoch races, and can a separate oracle detect each flaw?

- **Primary endpoint:** per-fault-family detection rate by the separate oracle for injected omitted-dependency mutants (write-skew family; policy-epoch race family), with propagation depth counted as admitted dependent writes downstream of the omission per independent history.
- **Strongest competing baseline:** B3 entity-OCC without semantic dependency closure — the ablation arm whose expected failures define the anomaly families; B2 PostgreSQL SERIALIZABLE provides the sound reference for whether SSI alone already detects the same races.
- **Independent oracle:** the separate dependency-closure/history oracle (Elle-based export adjudication); mutant injection is adjudicated exclusively by oracle evidence, never by introspecting candidate internals (`CASE_MATRIX.md` QA rule).
- **Statistical test:** exact binomial (Clopper–Pearson) confidence intervals on detection proportion per fault family; Fisher's exact test for arm-level detection contrasts; indeterminate outcomes counted in the denominator, never coerced to PASS.
- **Falsification condition:** any injected omitted-dependency flaw within a registered fault family that the separate oracle fails to detect blocks the closure-completeness claim; if the oracle cannot adjudicate without candidate internals, oracle independence is broken and the study stops and rebuilds the oracle (never repairs the oracle to match the candidate post hoc).

### RQ3 — Fanout and contention where dependency-scope overhead erases throughput gains

**RQ3 (blueprint):** At what graph fanout and contention does dependency-scope overhead erase throughput gains relative to PostgreSQL SERIALIZABLE?

- **Primary endpoint:** committed throughput (ops/sec) ratio of B4 candidate vs B2 as a function of dependency-graph fanout and worker contention (1/2/4/8/16 workers, Zipf skew, varying dependency density), with the crossover point defined as the smallest tested fanout/contention level whose ratio confidence interval includes 1; supporting metrics p50/p95/p99 latency and retry amplification.
- **Strongest competing baseline:** B2 PostgreSQL SERIALIZABLE (SSI) with schema-check predicates — the DB-native comparator whose performance defines the null-benefit boundary.
- **Independent oracle:** the same independent history checker validates exported histories at scale; PostgreSQL release/version and isolation setting plus kernel SHA are recorded with every run (blueprint evidence contract).
- **Statistical test:** paired bootstrap on per-setting throughput ratios (cluster = independent schedule/seed) with 95% confidence intervals; the crossover is reported as an interval over tested fanout levels, not a point estimate extrapolated beyond the grid; all performance claims come from real PostgreSQL runs.
- **Falsification condition:** if fully serializable PostgreSQL is as fast as the candidate across all tested fanout and contention levels, the performance-benefit claim is falsified and the null performance benefit is candidly reported (blueprint falsification rule); any PostgreSQL performance claim derived from SQLite observations is an automatic stop-and-revise violation.

## 5. Non-overlap controls (anti-salami, five-part distinction)

1. **Different precise question:** GLHS R4 asked whether co-versioned disclosure-to-commit binding governs longitudinal writes; this study asks whether automatically derived dependency closures reduce unnecessary aborts without forbidden commits under concurrency, and where their overhead breaks even (RQ1–RQ3). DSN 2027 asks crash-recoverability questions; PoPETs/PST/IFIP/ACNS/SACMAT/AIME/DIMVA ask unrelated questions per `CONTEXT/PREVIOUS_ANTI_OVERLAP_MATRIX.md`.
2. **Different primary endpoint:** false-stale rejection and committed throughput under benign contention with dependency scope (registry: `forbidden commit rate under dependency-complete schedules`) — not crash-consistency violation rate (DSN), replay acceptance (IFIP), or governed-commit correctness (GLHS).
3. **New independent inputs:** new synthetic schedule families (`synthetic-compsac-2027`), new dependency-closure oracle and benchmark arms B0–B4 on real PostgreSQL; no frozen prior dataset is executed again as new evidence.
4. **Prior work disclosed and cited:** Silo (`silo13`), Elle (`elle20`), Zanzibar (`zanzibar19`), Macaroons (`macaroons14`), PostgreSQL SSI (Ports & Grittner 2012), and the parent GLHS/HEALTHINF work are cited as the strongest competing explanations, never silently cannibalized.
5. **No archive as nine results:** one shared repository and background methods are acceptable; repackaging the same frozen experiment as a second paper is not.

### 5.1 DSN differentiation (mandatory)

DSN and COMPSAC are a recorded potential merge pair; this register refuses the merge by endpoint separation. The DSN study's primary endpoint is duplicate/lost durable effect after crashes — crash cut-points, recovery fault scheduling, and post-crash reconciliation. COMPSAC's endpoint is false-stale rejection and throughput under *benign contention* with dependency-scoped admission; no crash cut-points are injected, no recovery journal is built, and the concurrency schedules are disjoint from DSN's crash schedules by design (`CONTEXT/PREVIOUS_ANTI_OVERLAP_MATRIX.md`: "DSN needs crash cut-points; COMPSAC needs benign-contention and dependency scope"). A commit-correctness result here is not a recoverability result, and vice versa.

### 5.2 Frozen-archive refusal (mandatory)

The parent GLHS E15–E22 results and the accepted HEALTHINF paper are **background, not new venue experiments** (blueprint evidence contract). This register refuses, and downstream COMPSAC tasks must refuse: (a) counting frozen E15–E22 outcomes as new COMPSAC evidence; (b) reusing R4 SQLite observations as PostgreSQL performance — the source audit records E15–E19 backends as `sqlite:///:memory:`, so they cannot support any SSI/serializability claim; (c) reusing GovRed (RIVF) or GovMut (SOICT) frozen studies as COMPSAC results (`research/publication_registry.yaml` overlap statuses). Evidence for every COMPSAC claim must derive mechanically from `artifacts/venue2027/compsac/<run_id>/manifest.json` produced by this study's own frozen protocol.

## 6. Go/No-Go decision

Decision: GO

**Justification.** (1) COMPSAC is the program's priority venue (`priority_rank 1` in `MANIFESTS/priority_roadmap.csv`) and is a systems venue whose evaluation needs deterministic synthetic workloads and real PostgreSQL — **no model provider is needed**, so the dominant external dependency of the other venues is absent. (2) The required shared prerequisites have PASS reports on record (`proof/SH-claim_overlap.json`, `proof/SH-literature_hardening.json`) and the shared overlap registry passes its audit with `compsac.reuses_archive: []`. (3) The five-part anti-salami distinction is defensible: distinct question, distinct endpoint (dependency-derived safety/throughput frontier), new inputs, disclosed prior work, and explicit archive refusal (§5). (4) The falsification matrix (§3) names real, checkable prior work for every claim, so the study can fail honestly.

**Conditions attached to GO:** (a) B2 must run against real PostgreSQL; SQLite results may never substitute (declared friction, isolated container per execution contract); (b) `COMPSAC-prior` must register the PostgreSQL SSI primary source in the literature ledger as a verified baseline before any comparative claim freezes; (c) the closure oracle and candidate must remain import-disjoint, verified by `COMPSAC-oracle`; (d) if any §3 falsifying observation occurs, the register reopens (`SCOPE_REOPEN`) and dependent tasks re-plan rather than proceed.
