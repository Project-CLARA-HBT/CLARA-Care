# Does lexical/dense disagreement predict which score to trust across sources?

Use NanoArguAna, NanoFiQA2018 and NanoSCIDOCS as public development tasks. These deliberately small, relevance-enriched candidate corpora are not full BEIR. Rotate one entire dataset out for assessment and fit on the other two (equal weight per dataset). Reuse no judgments from the assessed dataset. Each query's unit score is nDCG@10 with exponential gains, unjudged documents assigned zero, known self-matches excluded, and ID ascending as deterministic tie-break. Include every labeled query. The upper bound uses the best member of the same convex weight grid for each query after seeing its labels; this diagnostic is never a deployable competitor.

Score the complete corpus for every query. BM25 uses lowercased Unicode alphanumeric words, k1=1.2 and b=0.75; MiniLM-L6-v2 uses attention-mask mean pooling and L2 normalization, title plus text, 256 tokens maximum, float32 on CUDA. Give every fusion method the same two score vectors. Use min-max normalization within query for convex weights. Evaluate lexical and dense single systems, ordinary RRF k=60, train-selected k in {10,30,60,100}, equal-weight convex fusion and a single trained convex weight (dense share 0,0.1,...,1). Candidate switches the trained weight according to whether the two rankers share at most two document IDs in their top ten. Fit a separate weight in each stratum using only the two training datasets, macro-averaging over sources that contain that stratum. Empty training stratum falls back to the global fit. Break training ties in favor of weights closest to 0.5 and then smaller numeric value. Fit an equal-capacity query-length split (up to ten BM25 tokens vs longer) as a competing partition. Low overlap can flag disagreement, but it cannot on its own identify which ranking is correct. The falsifiable hypothesis is that the direction of the weight change generalizes across these independent sources.

Retain all query scores, fit choices, wins and losses. A development advance requires mean nDCG gain of at least 0.02 against every deployable comparator and positive paired gains on each of the three sources. A query-level paired stratified bootstrap with 5,000 draws and seed 728 estimates uncertainty conditional on these three sources. RATINGS and raw text stay outside git. Any independent confirmation requires different source families and stronger neural estimators and adaptive fusion systems.

Bruch et al. already compare score-level convex fusion and reciprocal rank fusion and examine normalization and data efficiency. Cormack et al. introduced RRF. DAT already tunes the sparse/dense balance per query using external relevance assessment; the present hypothesis concerns precisely the transferability of an overlap-only signal without a judge. A gain over one single retriever supplies no generic hybrid-retrieval novelty.
